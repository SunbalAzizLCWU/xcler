Most small and mid-sized companies have tried ChatGPT. Far fewer have turned generative AI into a process that saves hours every week. The gap is rarely the technology; it is picking use cases where the **input is available, the output is checkable and the volume is real**.

Here are twelve that consistently pay off, with a rough sense of effort and risk.

![Grid of 12 generative AI use cases: quotes, support, product texts, contracts, recruiting, reporting and more](/blog/blog-genai-use-cases-grid.webp)

## Sales and customers

**1. Quote drafting.** Turn a customer email or call notes into a structured quote draft using your price list and templates. *Effort: medium. Risk: low with human approval.*

**2. Support replies.** Draft answers to incoming tickets from your knowledge base, for an agent to review and send. *Effort: medium. Risk: low.*

**3. Sales emails and follow-ups.** Personalised follow-ups based on CRM data and the last conversation. *Effort: low. Risk: low.*

**4. Product texts.** Generate and translate product descriptions from spec sheets for shop, marketplaces and catalogues. *Effort: low. Risk: low with style guide.*

## Operations and documents

**5. Contract review.** Flag unusual clauses, missing terms and deviations from your standard contract. *Effort: medium. Risk: medium — always with legal review.*

**6. Document extraction.** Pull fields from invoices, delivery notes and forms into your ERP. *Effort: medium. Risk: low with validation rules.*

**7. Reporting.** Turn weekly numbers into a written summary with highlights and anomalies. *Effort: low. Risk: low.*

**8. Translation and localisation.** Translate manuals, websites and emails with your terminology. *Effort: low. Risk: low with a glossary.*

## People and knowledge

**9. Recruiting.** Draft job ads, summarise applications against criteria, schedule interviews. *Effort: low. Risk: medium — keep final decisions human and follow anti-discrimination rules.*

**10. Onboarding.** An assistant that answers new employees' questions from your handbook and policies. *Effort: medium. Risk: low.*

**11. Internal knowledge base.** Search across wikis, drives and tickets with cited answers — see our guide to [RAG chatbots](/en/blog/rag-chatbot-company-knowledge). *Effort: medium to high. Risk: low.*

**12. Code assistance.** Help your IT team write scripts, integrations and tests faster. *Effort: low. Risk: low with review.*

## How to pick your first three

Score each candidate from 1 to 5 on:

- **Volume** — how often does it happen?
- **Time per case** — how long does it take today?
- **Checkability** — can a person quickly verify the output?
- **Data access** — is the input already digital and reachable?

Start with the highest total. In most companies that is support replies, document extraction or product texts.

## Three use cases in detail

### Support replies: from inbox to draft in seconds
**Before:** agents read each email, search the help centre and old tickets, and write a reply from scratch — often 5 to 10 minutes per ticket.

**After:** incoming tickets are classified, relevant help articles and past answers are retrieved, and a draft reply appears in the helpdesk with sources linked. The agent checks, edits and sends. Straightforward tickets take a minute; complex ones still get full attention.

**What you need:** a helpdesk with an API, a maintained knowledge base, clear tone guidelines and a feedback button for bad drafts.

### Document extraction: invoices and delivery notes
**Before:** accounting staff type supplier, date, amounts, VAT and order numbers from PDFs into the ERP.

**After:** a workflow reads every incoming document, extracts the fields with an AI model, checks them against purchase orders and plausibility rules, and books clean cases automatically. Only mismatches reach a person, with the problem highlighted.

**What you need:** a mailbox or upload folder as the entry point, ERP access, validation rules and a small exception queue. See the worked example in our [automation ROI guide](/en/blog/ai-automation-roi-calculation).

### Product texts: catalogue work at scale
**Before:** marketing writes product descriptions one by one, and translations lag weeks behind.

**After:** descriptions are generated from spec sheets using a style guide and approved examples, translated with a terminology glossary and pushed to the shop and marketplaces after review.

**What you need:** structured product data, a written style guide, a glossary and a review step for new product types.

## A 90-day roadmap

**Days 1–15: choose and prepare.** Score candidate use cases, pick one, record baseline metrics, check data protection and get access to the systems involved.

**Days 16–45: build and test.** Build the workflow with a human review step, test it on real historical cases and refine prompts, rules and data.

**Days 46–75: pilot.** Run it on live work with a small team. Measure time per case, error rate and user feedback every week.

**Days 76–90: decide and scale.** Compare with the baseline. If it works, roll it out to the full team and select the next use case; if not, fix the root cause or stop.

## Governance without bureaucracy

- **A short AI usage policy**: which tools are approved, which data may be used, who approves new use cases.
- **An inventory** of AI use cases with owner, purpose, data and provider.
- **Training** so staff know how to review AI output critically.
- **EU AI Act awareness**: most SME use cases are low-risk, but uses that affect people's rights — such as recruiting decisions — carry stricter obligations.

## Common mistakes

- Starting with the most impressive demo instead of the most frequent task.
- No owner in the business who measures the result.
- Letting AI send things to customers without review in the first months.
- Ignoring data protection: personal data into a US tool without a data processing agreement.
- Treating a prompt in a chat window as a finished solution instead of building it into the workflow.

## What it costs

A single use case built into your existing tools with n8n or Make typically takes one to four weeks. Model costs are usually small compared with the hours saved. The larger investment is clean data and a proper review step.

## FAQ

**Do we need our own AI model?**
Almost never. Commercial models with good prompts, your data via retrieval and clear rules cover most SME needs.

**Is generative AI GDPR-compliant?**
It can be: choose providers with EU processing, sign a data processing agreement, minimise personal data and document the use.

**How do we measure success?**
Time per case before and after, error rate and throughput. Measure for four weeks before you scale.

**Can employees just use ChatGPT on their own?**
Individual use can help, but without approved tools and rules staff may paste confidential or personal data into services without a data processing agreement. Provide an approved business tool and clear guidance.

**Which departments benefit first?**
Usually customer service, accounting, sales support and marketing, because they handle high volumes of text and documents.

**Do we need a data scientist?**
Rarely for these use cases. You need someone who understands the process, access to the systems and a partner or internal developer who can build the workflow.
